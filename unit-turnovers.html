Unit turnovers · IN · MI · WI

From the day they leave to the day it's ready to show.

A unit turnover is everything that happens between one tenant handing back the keys and the next one signing a lease. Sometimes that's a broom, a coat of paint and a weekend. Sometimes the last tenant packed what they wanted and walked out on the rest — furniture still in the rooms, food in the fridge, a garage you haven't opened yet.

Either way the unit isn't earning a dollar until it's ready. And most of what stands between you and a new lease isn't one trade. It's a haul-out, a clean, a painter, a floor guy and somebody to check the detectors — each with their own schedule, each waiting on the one before.

We do the whole stretch. Our own crew, one point of contact, one quote.

Three levels, depending on what it needs

1

Clear & Clean The unit is empty and earning

We empty it out and haul it away — furniture, appliances, mattresses, the garage, whatever they left. Then a deep clean top to bottom, smoke and CO detectors checked and replaced where they're dead or past date, and the locks rekeyed.

  • Haul-out and disposal
  • Deep clean
  • Smoke & CO detectors
  • Rekey
2

Paint & Refresh Everything in 1, plus it shows well

Everything above, and then the unit stops looking like somebody else's home. Walls patched and painted, the small broken things fixed, tired fixtures and hardware swapped out.

  • Patch & paint
  • Fixtures and hardware
  • Blinds and plates
  • Minor repairs
3

Layered Turnover Everything in 2, plus the real work

For the unit — or the building — that needs more than paint. Flooring, kitchen and bath, siding, roof, landscaping: you tell us what's on the list and we scope the whole thing as one job instead of four trades and four schedules.

  • Flooring
  • Kitchen & bath
  • Siding and roof
  • Grounds and landscaping
